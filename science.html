Science

The science behind AromaClip.

Aromatherapy is more than a pleasant scent — it's a practice with a long history and a growing body of research behind it. AromaClip follows a simple, familiar pathway: a microdose of essential oil, carried on a slow inhale, to the senses that help shape how we feel.

01

How AromaClip works

A micro-inhaler that clips to the nose for 10–15 minutes at a time — a gentle, measured inhale of its essential oils rather than a spray.

The device
Clips to one nostril for 10–15 minutes — breathe in its essential oils as often through the day as you like, but no more than once an hour.
The pathway
On the inhale, aroma molecules reach receptors in the nasal cavity — closely linked to the limbic system, the part of the brain associated with emotion, stress, sleep and memory.
The response
In the space of a slow inhale, many people notice a shift — in the depth of their breath, their tension, and their mood.

“Breath is one of the simplest ways to steady ourselves — and AromaClip makes it intentional.”

02

Why essential oils work

Research has long explored the qualities associated with essential oils — calming, sleep-supporting and attention-supporting, as well as antiseptic properties.

  • Lavender — lowers cortisol.
  • Citrus — stimulates dopamine and serotonin.
  • Eucalyptus — traditionally used for easier breathing.

“Scent is our oldest sense — and one of the gentlest ways to change how a moment feels.”

03

What the research shows

Lehrner et al., 2005
Lavender and orange aromas reduce anxiety and improve mood in dental patients.
Kiecolt-Glaser et al., 2008
Aromatherapy modulates the immune system and stress markers.
Moss et al., 2003
Rosemary aroma enhances memory and alertness.
